Ransomware Readiness Checklist for South African SMEs (2026)

South African businesses sit in one of the most heavily targeted regions globally. Here are seven concrete controls that separate companies that recover in hours from those that shut down.

Modern ransomware attacks rarely start with Hollywood-style hacking. Most begin with a realistic fake supplier invoice, a compromised Microsoft 365 login, or an unpatched workstation. Basic legacy antivirus alone is no longer enough to stop behavioral encryption attacks.

The 7-Point Readiness Checklist

  • 1. Enforce MFA on Every Account: Multi-Factor Authentication must be mandatory across Microsoft 365, VPNs, remote desktop tools, and accounting systems—with legacy authentication disabled.
  • 2. Replace Basic Antivirus with Behavioral EDR: Endpoint Detection & Response (EDR) monitors suspicious process behavior and isolates an infected machine before encryption spreads across your network.
  • 3. Automate OS & Third-Party Patching: Unpatched browsers, PDF readers, and Windows servers remain a top entry point. Use 24/7 RMM patch automation rather than manual updates.
  • 4. Maintain Immutable Offsite Backups: Attackers actively search for and delete local NAS drives and network backups. Your backups must be isolated and immutable so ransomware cannot encrypt them.
  • 5. Test Your Actual Restore Speed (RPO/RTO): A backup job that says "green" is useless if you have never verified how long a full server or workstation restore takes.
  • 6. Secure Email & Collaboration: Configure SPF, DKIM, and DMARC records and deploy advanced anti-phishing filtering to stop Business Email Compromise (BEC) and fake banking-detail changes.
  • 7. Train Staff on Real-World Phishing: Brief, recurring awareness training turns employees from your biggest risk into an early warning system.
