Key Distinction

Backup Is Not the Same as Disaster Recovery

Backup creates recovery points for protected data or systems. Disaster recovery focuses on restoring critical workloads and business operations when normal infrastructure is unavailable.

Some businesses need managed backup alone. Others may need a more structured disaster recovery solution. NAS assesses your requirements before recommending either service.

Realistic Expectations

What Affects Recovery Speed?

Recovery time depends on the environment and recovery method. NAS discusses realistic requirements before committing to a service.

Data Volume

The total amount of data to restore directly impacts initial download and restoration times.

Backup Frequency

Available recovery points dictate how recent the restored files or system image will be.

Internet Bandwidth

Cloud restoration speed relies on local connection stability and download capacity.

Replacement Hardware

Availability of compatible hardware or recovery infrastructure to receive restored volumes.

Application Complexity

Specific database and software dependency configurations required prior to operational startup.

Testing & Verification

Prior testing and configured recovery processes accelerate actual incident restoration.
